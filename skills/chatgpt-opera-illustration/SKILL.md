# Chinese Opera Editorial Illustration

Create original raster illustrations in the visual family demonstrated by the bundled references. This is art direction for ChatGPT Images, not a trained model, filter, animation engine, or guarantee of identical output.

## Choose the reference deliberately

Read [the reference index](references/index.md). Inspect `references/images/01-opera-pair.png` and one example close to the requested subject. Use those images as **style references**, not layouts to duplicate. For a continuing series, use its latest approved image as the primary reference and one bundled image as a secondary style anchor.

Separate image roles in the prompt: style reference, factual subject reference, and edit target. A photograph of an instrument should control its construction; the opera illustration should control color and mark-making. Do not let an unrelated photo change the whole rendering style.

## Art direction

- Let a few large, interlocking shapes carry the image. Give every scene one visual idea: an exchange between two people, a sleeve's sweep, a winding lake reflection, a single instrument.
- Use long, balanced silhouettes, small expressive profiles or restrained three-quarter faces, ivory skin shapes, a few eye/brow marks and coral cheeks. Avoid realistic portrait modeling, large doll eyes and crowded jewelry. Profile is a reliable starting choice, not a ban on other views.
- Draw water sleeves as broad cloth attached to a cuff, with a readable leading edge and two or three folds. Check shoulder → elbow → wrist → cuff → sleeve. Avoid disconnected ribbons or unexplained limbs.
- Keep most surfaces matte and nearly flat. Localize fine dry pigment grain and faint brocade to selected folds/edges; leave large calm areas. Refinement comes from curves, gesture, spacing and color relationships, not dense ornament or airbrushed depth.
- Work within this family, changing the dominant color per scene: ultramarine `#2c207b`, slate blue `#8ea9b5`, coral `#ed805f`, blush `#f6d3d0`, mustard `#edc866`, lilac `#aaa0ce`, ivory `#fffdf7`. These are direction, not a literal pixel-count restriction.
- Give the background a shape rhythm related to the subject. Avoid generic concentric arches, scattered decorative icons, plastic architecture and automatic fantasy embellishment.
- Do not force a plum blossom, pair of actors, moon or sleeve into every request. For a plum-led series, make the emblem five rounded petals, separated from nearby details and readable at thumbnail size.

## Generate and refine

Use the available ChatGPT image-generation tool. In Codex, use built-in `image_gen` when available, inspect local reference images first, and attach the chosen reference files to the generation call. Do not replace an illustration request with programmatic SVG figures. If image generation is unavailable, provide the prepared prompt and explain that generation has not occurred.

For a new composition, combine the style block and one subject brief from [prompt recipes](references/prompts.md). Specify the requested aspect ratio; portrait 3:4 suits chapter art, landscape suits ensembles and page illustrations. Make one composition per image. Keep text as an editable layout layer unless the user wants lettering inside the picture.

For edits, state exactly what changes and what stays: composition, faces, palette, gesture, texture. Save a sibling version. For a series, vary pose, framing, dominant color and subject while retaining shape language and texture scale. Approve the first keyframe before treating it as the series anchor.

Inspect the result at thumbnail and full size. Check attached shoes and hands, coherent props, recognizable silhouettes, selective texture, and breathing room. If a face or sleeve is wrong, edit that region with explicit invariants rather than redesigning the whole image. For motion-ready art, reserve room around the moving motif and keep its center unobscured; a static image is not a layered rig.

Treat the bundled scenes as interpretive art. They are not historical costume documentation, verified instrument diagrams or portraits of named performers. Use supplied factual references when those distinctions matter.
